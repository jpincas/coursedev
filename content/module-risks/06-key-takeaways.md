# Key Takeaways

You now understand both the power of AI and the discipline required to use it responsibly.

## The Five Critical Realities

**1. Performance isn't automatic.** Ninety-five percent of organisations see no measurable returns. The METR study showed developers were slower despite feeling faster. The technology works, but only with proper implementation.

**2. Hallucinations are structural.** Rates vary from 0.7% to 48% depending on model and domain. They won't disappear. Verification must be built into workflows, not treated as optional.

**3. Skills atrophy measurably.** The automation paradox is real. Doctors' unassisted detection rates fell after AI exposure. Regular practice without AI maintains the capability to verify AI outputs.

**4. Security requires architecture, not just training.** Thirty-eight percent of employees share confidential data despite training. Agents inherit file permissions and expose access you didn't know you had. Technical controls matter more than policies.

**5. You own the output legally.** "The AI said to" is not a defence. If you use AI output, you're responsible for its accuracy, its impact, and its legal status. Human creative control determines copyright protection.

## What Changes on Monday

You've learned the technical skills: context engineering, proper prompting, file-based workflows, delegation patterns, verification techniques.

Now you understand the boundaries:
- Verify AI outputs for critical decisions using the three-check rule
- Maintain your core skills through regular unassisted practice
- Classify data before AI processing and use appropriate tool tiers
- Exercise creative control to establish legal authorship
- Build verification into workflows rather than assuming accuracy

## Putting It All Together

The risks you've learned here aren't reasons to avoid AI. They're the operating manual for using it effectively.

The organisations succeeding with AI aren't the ones with the best technology. They're the ones with the best discipline.
