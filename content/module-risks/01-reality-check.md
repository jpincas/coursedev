# The Honest Assessment

AI works. But not in the way most organisations expect.

Studies suggest **the vast majority of organisations** see no measurable returns from AI investments. McKinsey reports only **1% of companies** have reached AI maturity. Sixty-seven percent of AI workflow projects fail within the first six months.

These numbers matter because they separate the hype from reality. The technology has crossed genuine capability thresholds. But deployment success isn't automatic.

## The Performance Paradox

A July 2025 randomised controlled trial by METR (Model Evaluation and Threat Research) tested 16 experienced open-source developers. The study measured their actual performance on real-world coding tasks using AI assistance tools.

**They were 19% slower when using AI coding tools.**

But they believed they were 20% faster.

This reveals a dangerous cognitive bias. The tools made the work feel easier — the friction of typing, looking up syntax, and thinking through implementation details was reduced. But that perceived ease didn't translate to actual productivity. The time saved on mechanical tasks was consumed by reviewing AI suggestions, correcting errors, and understanding code the AI had written.

Critically, **the developers couldn't accurately perceive this gap.** The subjective experience of ease masked the objective reality of slower completion times.

## The Business Case Isn't Automatic

Harvard Business School conducted a randomised controlled trial with 640 entrepreneurs building actual businesses. One group had access to AI tools. The control group did not.

The result after monitoring business outcomes over time: **no statistical difference in business performance** between the two groups.

No difference in revenue. No difference in growth. No difference in customer acquisition. Access to AI, by itself, produced no measurable business advantage.

But within the AI group, there was significant variation. Some entrepreneurs outperformed others dramatically.

The practitioners who did benefit had one thing in common: **they had better judgement about which AI advice to follow and which to reject.**

The value came from their domain expertise informing when to trust the AI, when to question it, and when to ignore it completely. AI amplified their judgement. For those without good judgement, it amplified bad decisions just as effectively.

## What Actually Works

This isn't doom-and-gloom. It's calibration.

The organisations and individuals seeing genuine productivity gains share specific traits:

- They start small with high-frequency tasks
- They iterate relentlessly rather than expecting perfection
- They verify everything critical
- They maintain their own skills alongside AI assistance
- Most importantly: they redesign their work around AI capabilities rather than inserting AI into existing workflows

**The technology has crossed a genuine capability threshold.** You've learned how to use it effectively across this course — context engineering, proper delegation, file-based workflows, verification techniques.

The question isn't whether AI works. The question is whether you'll use it with the discipline these numbers demand.
